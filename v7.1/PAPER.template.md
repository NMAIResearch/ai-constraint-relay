# AI Infrastructure: Commitments, Delivery and Access

*Selected evidence from the US, EU and UK · v7.1*

This work was produced through an AI-assisted workflow directed by the author.

## 1. Commitments and access

<!-- D01 -->
The selected records connect money to specific rights and obligations: credit support accompanies a supplier restriction, customer deposits support future supply, and electricity access carries continuing generation conditions. They also record equipment shipments, rising supplier sales and available services.

**Inference:** the useful distinction is between securing a position in future capacity and obtaining productive capacity. The evidence supports that distinction at the contract and project level; it does not establish an industry-wide causal relay.

<!-- D02 -->
This assessment follows identifiable commitments through financing, equipment delivery and permission to operate. It covers selected US relationships and an EU/UK extension, using records assembled through 14 September 2026. Each observation retains its reporting period. The selection is purposive, with no representative sample, regional performance ranking or aggregate estimate of AI investment. A June financial statement remains a June observation when retrieved in September.

<!-- D03 -->
The contribution is a comparison of what different instruments secure and what remains contingent after agreement. Ordinary project finance is a necessary comparison: guarantees, customer commitments and residual-value protections do not, by themselves, establish exceptional AI fragility. No matched baseline comparison was run.

**Inference:** the records can identify contractual exposure and allocation without determining whether the arrangements are unusually risky or whether one constraint caused another.

## 2. Financing secures rights before service begins

<!-- D04 -->
NVIDIA's disclosed SB Energy arrangement pairs a conditional USD{{C021}}B guarantee cap with NVIDIA infrastructure exclusivity, subject to exceptions, for an initial {{C023}}GW of IT load. Guarantees begin with lease commencement in {{C024:word}} phases, first expected in NVIDIA fiscal 2029. Defined tenant defaults can trigger payment; exposure declines and a satisfactory-credit-rating termination condition applies. The cap is neither cash paid nor expected loss.

**Inference:** the arrangement links credit support to future hardware choice. Its necessity for financing, and its effect relative to a project without exclusivity, remain unverified. [NVIDIA filing, subsequent event and risk terms](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm); [SB Energy project account](https://sbenergy.com/nvidia-ai-compute-ports-pike-ohio/).

<!-- D05 -->
NVIDIA also discloses separate AI-cloud purchase commitments. Providers can redirect capacity to third parties on the specified terms; qualifying use reduces commitments, and conditional revenue sharing applies. NVIDIA nevertheless warns that it may lack demand or the ability to use or resell capacity it must buy. Contractual flexibility and residual demand exposure coexist. [NVIDIA filing, cloud agreements and associated risks](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm).

<!-- D06 -->
CoreWeave's August announcement describes a USD{{C038}}B facility with an approximately {{C039:word}}-year term, against customer contracts averaging {{C040:word}} years. The same release expressly allows renewal or re-leasing, subject to credit-agreement criteria, and reports Moody's Ba2 and Fitch BB+ ratings. Nscale separately reports investment-grade ratings for its delayed-draw US facilities, without naming the agencies in the inspected release. These are borrower disclosures; independent rating reports were not inspected. [CoreWeave facility](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Closes-2-6-Billion-Loan-Facility-Expanding-Financing-Flexibility-for-AI-Infrastructure/default.aspx); [Nscale facilities](https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-3-billion-delayed-draw-term-loans).

<!-- D07 -->
**Inference:** CoreWeave's term difference locates a renewal question, but does not measure a financing deficit. Renewal permission is valuable; it does not supply the future customer, price or cash needed to meet obligations. A conclusion about refinancing stress would require the repayment schedule and operating receipts, not simply subtraction of average contract terms.

<!-- D08 -->
Meta's June interim filing illustrates a different allocation. Hyperion property leases have {{C049:word}}-year initial terms, with options extending the total term up to {{C050}} years. A declining residual-value guarantee threshold of about USD{{C051}}B is conditional on termination or non-renewal, asset values and other terms. Meta reports that payment is not probable and records no liability for that guarantee. Those are unaudited interim disclosures.

**Inference:** a contingent threshold identifies retained exposure; it is not an expected loss or evidence of improper accounting. [Meta filing, Hyperion arrangements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm).

<!-- D09 -->
These instruments cannot be added into a common cash total. A guarantee cap, a loan facility, a future purchase and a residual-value threshold have different triggers and timing.

**Inference:** the transaction-level question is which party must perform, pay or find another customer when circumstances change. Headline commitment size alone does not answer it.

## 3. Customer funding meets manufacturing and delivery

<!-- D10 -->
Modine received a USD{{C061}}M customer deposit in March for more than USD{{C062}}B of expected sales during 2027 to 2029. Those expected purchases are not guaranteed. Its April to June 2026 quarter also recorded production constraints, while Data Centers sales reached USD{{C065}}M against USD{{C066}}M a year earlier; guidance was reaffirmed. The constrained quarter preceded the funded customer's delivery window. It therefore does not establish a missed delivery to that customer. [Modine annual filing, customer agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/67347/000110465926066795/mod-20260331x10k.htm); [quarterly operating results](https://investors.modine.com/news/news-details/2026/Modine-Reports-First-Quarter-Fiscal-2027-Results/default.aspx).

<!-- D11 -->
**Inference:** the deposit is evidence of a customer financing access to future cooling supply. The simultaneous increase in supplier sales is evidence against treating production constraints as a complete stop. Whether funding delivers the contracted customer outcome must be assessed against that customer's dates and acceptance terms; the supplier's aggregate difficulties cannot substitute for that comparison.

<!-- D12 -->
Micron's June disclosures describe take-or-pay supply agreements, generally running through 2030, and USD{{C074}}B of expected deposits and related commitments, including about USD{{C075}}B in cash deposits. Expected receipts are not confirmed cash received. Management describes deposits as financing cash flows, outside free cash flow, with later returns to customers. It links the agreements to supply assurance and pricing visibility, alongside memory supply pressure and HBM allocation demands. Full customer contracts and current receipt balances were not inspected. [Micron filing, supply agreements and risks](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000015/mu-20260528.htm); [prepared remarks, financing treatment](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf).

<!-- D13 -->
**Inference:** customer commitments can support expansion and reserve access without removing manufacturing or performance risk. They also create obligations for the buyer. Supply assurance is an economic benefit, so prepayment should not be presented only as transferred risk. Neither a supplier's allocation account nor a contract announcement measures the economy-wide effect of HBM demand.

<!-- D14 -->
Physical expansion has intermediate milestones. Lumentum's announced NVIDIA partnership is non-exclusive; its acquired North Carolina site is to ramp InP laser production in mid-2028. SK hynix's August Indiana announcement targets HBM mass production in the second half of 2029, compared with the second half of 2028 in the earlier official announcement. That is a comparison of {{C089:word}} announcements, N={{C089}}, not an identified cause of delay. The stated Indiana process packages and tests wafers made in Korea. [Lumentum partnership](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-strategic-partnership-with-lumentum-to-develop-state-of-the-art-optics-technology); [plant programme](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-Announces-New-U-S--Manufacturing-Facility-to-Produce-Advanced-Lasers-for-the-Worlds-Largest-AI-Data-Centers/default.aspx); [SK hynix milestones](https://news.skhynix.com/en/groundbreaking-ceremony-in-indiana/); [historical official target](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/08/us-department-commerce-announces-preliminary-terms-sk-hynix-advance-us-ai-0).

<!-- D15 -->
GE Vernova's second-quarter report separates new reservations, firm orders and shipments: {{C091}}GW of new reservations, {{C092}}GW of direct orders, {{C093}}GW converted from reservations to orders, and {{C094}}GW shipped. Firm backlog rose from {{C095}}GW to {{C096}}GW. The reported reservation movements leave a {{C_GE_DERIVED_RESIDUAL}}GW unreconciled residual against the closing balance; it is not labelled cancellation here. These records cover Gas Power equipment beyond AI customers. They do not establish an individual project's wait or energisation date. [GE Vernova filed results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996810/000199681026000147/gevpressrelease2q26.htm).

<!-- D16 -->
The record also includes commercial output. Broadcom reports USD{{C100}}B in quarterly AI semiconductor revenue, combining custom accelerators and networking. That combined amount does not measure displaced NVIDIA GPUs. Epoch's Abilene directory supports partial operation using company records, imagery and engineering assumptions; it is not a meter of current workload utilisation.

**Inference:** operating evidence prevents the whole build-out from being classified as undelivered, while leaving completion and economic performance at particular sites unresolved. [Broadcom results](https://investors.broadcom.com/node/64671/pdf); [Abilene directory](https://epoch.ai/data/ai-data-centers/directory/openai-stargate-abilene); [Epoch methodology](https://epoch.ai/data/data-centers-documentation/methodology).

## 4. Access depends on policy as well as equipment

<!-- D17 -->
Ireland provides a concrete continuing obligation. EirGrid's May process implements the regulator's policy for new transmission-system applications of at least {{C106}} MVA received on or after 12 December 2025. Under that process, nominated generation must remain operational and meet the specified relationship between de-rated export capacity and the datacentre's maximum import capacity. If that generation fails or is withdrawn, a credible plan for immediate replacement is required; otherwise import capacity may be reduced or disconnected. Continuing renewable compliance is also required. The regulator's wider policy differentiates requirements by project size. The May document records pending judicial review proceedings concerning the underlying policy and warns that their outcome could change the process. Their subsequent outcome is unverified here. These are inspected process provisions, not verified outcomes for a particular customer's agreement. [EirGrid process, PDF pages 3 to 5 and 8](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Data-Centre-Connection-Offer-Process-and-Policy-V3-May-2026.pdf#page=4); [CRU decision, size-dependent requirements](https://cruie-live-96ca64acab2247eca8a850a7e54b-5b34f62.divio-media.com/documents/CRU2025236_Large_Energy_User_connection_policy_decision_paper.pdf).

<!-- D18 -->
**Inference:** this connection pathway makes supporting generation an obligation that can survive energisation. Financing the datacentre alone does not satisfy it. That is a specified dependency with an identified consequence for access, rather than a general prediction of electricity scarcity. The same process enables qualifying connections; it does not establish a blanket datacentre prohibition.

<!-- D19 -->
PJM's US proposals have a different status. Its January policy direction preceded the later auction shortfall. The July Reliability Backstop Procurement filing used an initial target of {{C118:comma}}MW UCAP, subject to qualifying reductions. Its proposed USD{{C119}}/MW-day UCAP limit applies to the MW-weighted average levelised procurement cost, not every offer. The separate August Interim Resource Adequacy Service proposal drew a Market Monitor protest. The inspected September backstop answer still requested acceptance; final Commission disposition is unverified. These records cannot be presented as an adopted universal connection restriction. [July filing](https://www.pjm.com/pjmfiles/directory/etariff/FercDockets/9806/20260731-er26-3380-000.pdf); [Monitor protest](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/filings/2026/IMM_Protest_Docket_No_ER26-3515_20260903.pdf); [September answer](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/documents/ferc/filings/2026/20260910-er26-3380-000.pdf).

<!-- D20 -->
In Great Britain, NESO reports connection offers issued through reform; government AI Growth Zone policy separately describes priority and self-build intentions. Neither record establishes a named datacentre's energisation. Equipment permission is another distinct condition: NVIDIA reports that Chinese restrictions limited licensed H200 sales, while some shipments occurred. That is the issuer's operating account, not a verification of current law. [NESO update](https://www.neso.energy/news/connecting-grid-has-work-investment-not-hold-it-back); [Growth Zone policy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/delivering-ai-growth-zones/delivering-ai-growth-zones); [NVIDIA reporting-period account](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm).

<!-- D21 -->
The EU gigafactory call adds public demand to the financing picture. EuroHPC and participating states intend to purchase compute access from selected consortia to support private investment. The inspected call remained open, with a 12 November 2026 deadline. A tender establishes a procurement route, not an award, paid expenditure or operating facility. Separately, EuroHPC's large-scale industrial access programme offers an application route through technical and peer review. It does not establish an allocation for every applicant. [Gigafactory procurement model](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/eurohpc-joint-undertaking-launches-ai-gigafactories-call-2026-07-30_en); [tender](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/call-tenders-selection-artificial-intelligence-gigafactory-consortia-and-establishment-ai_en); [industrial access programme](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/large-scale-access-ai-factories_en).

<!-- D22 -->
**Inference:** public procurement can influence who obtains infrastructure funding, while allocation programmes can give eligible users a route to compute without financing a private cluster. These channels belong in a money-and-access assessment alongside private capital and the proposed US grid backstops described above. The mechanisms procure different services: compute access and electricity resource adequacy. They cannot yet demonstrate delivered gigafactory capacity or establish a regional performance ranking.

## 5. European location and supply-chain dependence

<!-- D23 -->
The European cases separate the location of a service from its hardware, ownership and funding.

**Inference:** sovereignty is too coarse a label for these distinct dependencies. Local operation can have value without establishing local chip production or exclusively domestic capital. The relevant comparison identifies which layer changes in each project.

<!-- D24 -->
BT and Nscale's April announcement plans deployment across {{C140:word}} existing UK sites using NVIDIA infrastructure. The inspected record does not establish operating acceptance. In Portugal, Nscale's May Sines expansion announcement identifies EUR{{C142}}M for shared infrastructure and EUR{{C143}}M for a second {{C144}}MW building, linked to later deployment for Microsoft. Announced investment is not paid expenditure, and building capacity is not measured IT utilisation. Nscale's US loan facilities are not assigned to either European programme. [BT partnership](https://newsroom.bt.com/bt-to-work-with-nscale-to-accelerate-uk-sovereign-ai-capability-powered-by-nvidia/); [Sines expansion](https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-start-campus).

<!-- D25 -->
Mistral reports a EUR{{C148}}B Series D led by Samsung in September. It separately describes European Compute Units that exchange multi-year customer commitments for infrastructure access. The inspected material does not assign all equity proceeds to infrastructure or disclose the complete customer contract. Mistral reports regional inference endpoints available, while retaining specified exceptions for safeguarded subprocessor transfers outside the region. The operating endpoint and the proposed capacity expansion are different claims. [Financing statement](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-makes-sovereign-open-weight-ai-to-frontier/); [regional services and compute commitments](https://mistral.ai/news/regional-inference-open-models-new-compute/).

<!-- D26 -->
**Inference:** these projects can diversify service location while retaining external technology and financing relationships. That does not by itself invalidate their operational benefits. Equally, regional branding cannot establish supply-chain independence. The Indiana packaging example presents the same analytical distinction outside Europe: adding a domestic production stage can leave an upstream overseas dependency.

## 6. The political pacing debate

<!-- D27 -->
The September debate concerns proposed restraint and oversight of frontier development. It is relevant to infrastructure decisions, but its evidence currently consists of stated positions rather than demonstrated changes in orders, training schedules or investment. The political statements below have a separate support status from connection rules and procurement notices.

<!-- D28 -->
Dario Amodei's original essay distinguishes pacing from halting training or technical progress and proposes external evaluators, democratic coordination and wider coordination. ITV reports support from Elon Musk and Sam Altman, with Altman making a more specific commitment on evaluator access. Amodei's text is a primary statement; the Musk and Altman accounts remain secondary because their original posts were not retrieved. None establishes implementation or a private motive. [Amodei essay](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier); [ITV report](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-09-12/musk-and-altman-back-calls-to-slow-down-development-of-ai).

<!-- D29 -->
ABC reports Donald Trump opposing new restraints and invoking competition with China. Its publisher date is 15 September; the report was retrieved on 14 September UTC. The original Truth Social post was not retrieved. This is attributed political reporting, not a new legal instrument. [ABC report](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-15/trump-says-a-high-iq-president-is-the-only-guardrail-ai-needs/107153326).

<!-- D30 -->
**Inference:** slower frontier deployment could coexist with spending on inference, evaluation and safety infrastructure. It could also alter demand for particular facilities. The direction and scale cannot be read from endorsements alone. Evidence of changed purchase commitments, project schedules or operative conditions would be needed to connect the political disagreement to the financial relationships assessed here.

## 7. Findings and observations that could change them

<!-- D31 -->
The cases establish several concrete allocations: NVIDIA credit support accompanies a technology restriction; CoreWeave has contractual renewal and re-leasing options; Modine has received customer funding before the specified sales window; Micron reports commitments aimed at supply assurance; Ireland specifies a continuing condition for electricity access; and the EU call proposes public compute purchases.

**Inference:** these findings locate where future performance matters after money is committed. They extend a shortage inventory into an account of named counterparties, contractual conditions and delivery stages.

## Verification and limitations

**Verification.** Source links identify the issuing documents or expressly attributed reporting. The accompanying source catalogue records issuing URLs, reporting periods, inspected locations and evidence limits. Most commercial evidence is supplied by interested companies. Selected extracts, rather than every complete filing or agreement, were inspected. No bank reconciliation, site inspection, customer acceptance audit or matched project-finance baseline was completed. The statement section includes secondary reporting and does not independently establish implementation.
